# Privacy policy

_Effective October 29, 2026_

Locus - Genome Viewer ("Locus") is a free app for iPhone and iPad made by
Thad Judkins. This policy explains what happens to information when you use it.

## The short version

- Locus doesn't collect, store, or sell personal data. I don't run any
  servers for it.
- There are no accounts, analytics, advertising, or tracking.
- Genome searches and data requests go directly from your device to the UCSC
  Genome Browser.
- Everything Locus saves stays on your device, and you can erase it at any
  time.

## Information sent to UCSC

To show genome data, Locus sends requests directly to the UCSC Genome Browser
(genome.ucsc.edu and api.genome.ucsc.edu), run by the University of
California, Santa Cruz. Depending on what you do, these requests include:

- the genes, identifiers, or coordinates you search for;
- the genomic regions and tracks you view;
- DNA sequences you submit for BLAT alignment;
- the shared-session link and UCSC API key you enter, if you use those
  features.

Like any website, UCSC receives your device's IP address with each request.
UCSC handles this information under its own policies, described in the
[UCSC Genome Browser conditions of use](https://genome.ucsc.edu/conditions.html).
Locus doesn't send this information anywhere else.

## Information stored on your device

Locus saves the following on your device so it can pick up where you left off
and work with a poor connection:

- your workspaces, track choices, and layout settings;
- recent searches;
- cached genome data for regions you've viewed (kept for up to seven days);
- your UCSC API key, in the iOS Keychain;
- recent crash and hang reports from Apple's MetricKit (the newest five).

None of this leaves your device unless you choose to send it, as described
below. **••• → About & Help → Erase app data** deletes all of it, including
the Keychain items that iOS otherwise keeps after an app is deleted.

## Crash reports and support email

If you contact support from the app, Locus opens an email that includes your
app version, iOS version, and device model. If the app has crashed recently,
it asks whether to attach the saved crash reports. Those reports describe
where the app failed. They contain no genomic data or personal information.
You can review the email and change or remove anything before sending it.
I use what you send only to answer your request and fix problems.

If you've turned on **Share With App Developers** in iOS Settings → Privacy &
Security → Analytics & Improvements, Apple may also share crash reports and
usage statistics with me. Apple controls that setting and the data it
includes.

## Children

Locus is a scientific reference tool. It isn't directed at children and
doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone.
